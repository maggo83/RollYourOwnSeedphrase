Use this printed guide for a real seed phrase. If you are unsure about the method, first use the online guide or make a dry run with a dummy phrase. Keep rolls, bits, and seed words off networked devices.

1. Get plain, well-made six-sided dice plus a clean shoe box with a well-fitting lid or an opaque
   food-storage box of similar size. Avoid decorative, novelty, damaged, or visibly uneven dice.
   Don't use more dice than can move freely in the chosen container. A large dice cup and hard
   rolling tray can be used as an alternative.
   
2. Choose 12 or 24 words and one encoding before rolling. Base-4 works with either length. With
   regular dice, use 24 words for binary quantization.
   Print the matching page of BitsToWords.pdf.
   (Fill the Identifier with a name so you later know which seed this is.)
   Each numbered larger rectangle represents a word. Write the word in the lower, wider rectangle.
   The 11 smaller rectangles above are bits; each contains 0 or 1. First fill the bits using dice,
   then derive the words from the bits.
3. Print BIP39_Wordlist_Binary_Decimal_Searchable.pdf
   This will help us derive the words from the bits.
   Important: Do print this out. Do NOT search in this file on your desktop for the bits you have
   rolled. Not even partially.
4. Use a private room without cameras, phones, smart glasses, observers, or other recording devices.

5. Choose one of these two bit encodings before rolling:

   A. Base-4 rejection sampling (recommended compromise; fewer expected rolls):
      If the die is 1: enter "00" into the next two free bit rectangles
      If the die is 2: enter "01" into the next two free bit rectangles
      If the die is 3: enter "10" into the next two free bit rectangles
      If the die is 4: enter "11" into the next two free bit rectangles
      If the die is 5 or 6: ignore it completely

      You need exactly 64 accepted dice for 12 words or 128 accepted dice for 24 words.
      With ideal dice, this takes 96 respectively 192 physical dice results on average. The actual
      number varies.

   B. Binary quantization (no rejected results; fixed number of rolls):
      For 12 words, use casino-grade dice. With regular dice, choose 24 words or use base-4.

      If the die is 1, 2 or 3: enter "0" into the next free bit rectangle
      If the die is 4, 5 or 6: enter "1" into the next free bit rectangle

      You need exactly 128 dice results for 12 words or 256 for 24 words.

6. Depending on your setup:

   A. Big box with a lid:
      For every batch, put all dice into the box and close the lid. Shake it vigorously for at least
      ten hard shakes. Each shake should make the dice travel and collide audibly inside; if they
      are crowded and cannot move freely, use fewer dice or a larger box. Put the box down and
      gently tip it so the settled dice slide toward one edge. Then open the lid and read them from
      left to right; if some form a second row, read front to back. Do not move the dice into a line
      by hand. The tipping step only creates the reading order and does not replace the vigorous
      shaking.

      If any die is cocked or cannot be read unambiguously, discard the entire batch regardless of
      the visible values and shake the complete batch again.

   B. Cup and tray:
      Shake vigorously, cast with enough room and energy for several impacts, and read the dice
      where they stop using a spatial rule chosen before the first roll.

      Read every die using the fixed rule and apply the encoding selected in step 5. With base-4,
      faces 5 and 6 consume no bit rectangles;

   Repeat until you have filled all bit rectangles except the ones behind the last bold "|" of the
   last word.
   For the 12-word sheet this means all but the last 4; for the 24-word sheet, all but the last 8.
   In the final batch, use results in the predetermined order only until this boudnary and ignore
   all later dice.
   Never choose which results to retain based on their values.

7. For each word, look for the word that has the same 11 bits as you rolled in your printout of
   BIP39_Wordlist_Binary_Decimal_Searchable.pdf
   The first three bits of the word tell you which page you need to look into.
   The next three bits of the word tell you which column of that page you need to look into.
   The last five bits of the word tell you which row of that page you need to look into.

8. For the last word there are still some bits missing (4 bits for 12 words, 8 bits for 24 words).
   To find them you will need the help of a hardware wallet.
   (Because they are checksum, i.e. they are fully determined by the other bits. The computation is
   quite complex, but does not affect your entropy/randomness).
   The existing bits you have rolled limit the options to 16 (i.e. half a column for 12 words) / 256
   (one page for 24 words) words.
   You probably do not want to try all of these manually.
   How to do this will depend on your concrete HW wallet.
   Usually you enter the first eleven words as you normally would when entering an existing
   seedphrase and then the hw wallet will propose possible last words that lead to a valid checksum.
   Choose one of those that is in the list of options you determined.
